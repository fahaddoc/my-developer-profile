# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three audiences land on shahfahad.dev, usually from a LinkedIn post, a GitHub profile, a resume link or an Upwork proposal:

- **Remote hiring managers and recruiters** filling senior frontend / Flutter roles. They have a few minutes, often on a laptop between calls, and want proof fast: real shipped products, the merged Flutter and Supabase pull requests, the resume, a way to get in touch.
- **Freelance clients** (Upwork and direct). They want to see products that actually shipped, judge craft, and send a project inquiry.
- **Developers and peers** (Flutter and React community) arriving from a post or a PR. They come for the open-source work and stay if the site itself is impressive engineering.

Many arrive on a phone (LinkedIn app, in-app browser), so the mobile experience is a first-class surface, not a fallback.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Shah Fahad, a senior software engineer in Karachi (React, Next.js, Flutter, WebRTC; 8+ years). The site is itself the strongest work sample: a 3D space journey (Intro → About → Skills → Projects → Open Source → Experience → Contact) that shows frontend and 3D craft while carrying the facts a hiring decision needs.

Success: a visitor remembers the site, understands within one screen who Shah is and what he builds, finds the proof (projects, merged PRs, resume) without hunting, and reaches out.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, precise, confident. Calm and expensive rather than loud: slow deliberate camera moves, large editorial type, lots of deep space, one glowing accent. The emotion is "this person is serious about craft" with a hint of wonder. Facts stay honest and specific; the drama comes from presentation, never from inflated claims.

Reference feel (borrow the feeling, not the layout): Apple product launch pages (pacing, scale, restraint), Active Theory (cinematic WebGL that still reads as a product).

## Anti-references

- The template developer portfolio: skill bars, identical icon-card grids, a tiny uppercase eyebrow above every section, "Hi, I'm X 👋" heroes.
- Neon cyberpunk clichés: purple/pink glow everywhere, fake terminals, "hacker" scanlines as decoration.
- Gray-on-black text a recruiter has to squint at.
- Heavy 3D that stutters or drains battery on a mid-range Android phone.

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the proof.** Every effect must read as deliberate engineering, not decoration; if it would look like a template or a demo reel, cut it.
2. **Facts at a glance, wonder on the way.** The journey is cinematic, but who/what/proof/contact are reachable in one move from anywhere.
3. **One voice, two worlds.** Dark is deep space; light is the same journey over a daylight sea, islands where the planets were. Same stations, copy and type; only the world changes.
4. **Honest drama.** Big type and slow light do the persuading; copy stays specific and verifiable (PR numbers, shipped products, real dates).
5. **Fast is part of premium.** Quality tiers, touch-gated effects and reduced-motion paths are part of the design, not an afterthought.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: body text ≥4.5:1 and large text ≥3:1 in both themes, visible focus states, keyboard reachable navigation, a crawlable text alternative to the WebGL canvas (already present in SeoContent). Every animation has a `prefers-reduced-motion` path (crossfade or instant). Touch devices get native scroll and lighter effects.
